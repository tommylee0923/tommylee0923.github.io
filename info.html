About Me

I work at the intersection of architecture, engineering, and software. My background is in civil engineering and architecture, but my focus is on building tools around AEC workflows, making design data more usable, auditable, and automatable.

I'm interested in software-focused roles where design knowledge and software development meet: AEC Software Engineer, Design Technologist, BIM Developer, or Computational Designer.

Why AEC Software

Through architectural training and professional work, I became increasingly aware that many design and construction problems are not design problems, but tooling problems: data loss between platforms, inconsistent models, manual QA, and brittle workflows.

Learning to code with scripting and gradually moving into structured software development have changed how I approach these issues. Instead of working around limitations, I began building small tools to reduce friction, automate checks, and make models more reliable.

How I Work

  • Design-aware software thinking: I build tools with an understanding of how architects and engineers actually model, not just how APIs expect data to look.
  • Incremental, testable development: I prefer small, composable tools with clear inputs and outputs rather than monolithic systems.
  • Learning through building: Most of my technical growth comes from personal projects that solve real problems.

Looking Ahead

I'm focused on growing further into a role focused on AEC software development, design automation, and platform-level workflows while staying grounded in architectural and engineering practice. Much of my recent work focuses on BIM- and IFC-based tooling, including model validation, automation, and data quality checks, which are documented throughout this site.
